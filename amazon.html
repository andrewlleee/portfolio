Overview

Improving throughput across three connected departments.

As an Operations & Area Manager Intern at Amazon's SBD1 facility, I led a process improvement project spanning Manual Palletize, Robotics, and Outbound Dock — three departments that hand work off to each other in sequence, meaning a bottleneck in one almost always surfaced as a problem in the next. I spent eight weeks on the floor observing workflows, interviewing associates and managers across shifts, and implementing targeted fixes to close the gaps I found.

Approach

Start on the floor, not the spreadsheet.

Before proposing anything, I spent significant time observing both Front-Half and Back-Half night shifts to understand why one consistently outperformed the other, and conducted structured interviews with associates and Process Assistants across every role in the chain — scanners, RWC operators, waterspiders, and Quality Problem Solve. The goal was to separate what looked like a bottleneck from what actually was one.

Initiatives
Scanning Station Redesign
Replaced floor-mounted, tape-and-paper QR codes with durable, hip-level holders built from recycled materials — reducing misscans and the downstream inventory errors they caused.
RWC Headstart Program
Secured approval for a 15-minute early start for robotics operators to stage UPP and clear overnight issues, improving shift startup efficiency and cutting mid-shift disruptions.
Training Capacity Expansion
Built and presented the case to expand the site's trained-operator cap for robotics, working with the Learning Manager to raise it from 13 to as many as 22 depending on shift — directly easing a chronic understaffing constraint.
Waterspider Labor Tracking
Introduced a scanning requirement that converted an unmeasured, indirect role into a trackable one — giving leadership real-time visibility and accountability data that hadn't existed before.
Data

The rate climbed as recirc barely moved.

Tracking BHN's Manual Palletize rate against its recirculation percentage over the project window showed the improvement wasn't just associates working harder under pressure — the rate climbed roughly 45% from April to June while recirc rose only about 3 points, meaning throughput was genuinely improving rather than trading one problem for another.

Reflection

Not every idea survives contact with the floor.

I also piloted mobile hip printers and handscanners in MP and RWC, expecting they'd speed up label printing. In practice, operators and waterspiders pushed back — the added equipment weight was physically demanding, and operators actually preferred handling label printing themselves during natural idle time rather than waiting on a waterspider to do it for them. I ended the trial in those departments and redirected the same technology to Pallet Receive, where it did work — replacing a single handwritten label with a four-sided printed system that measurably improved accuracy for outbound drivers. The failed trial wasn't wasted; it told me exactly where the idea did and didn't fit.
